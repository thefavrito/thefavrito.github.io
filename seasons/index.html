What the network normally looks like, month by month

Each month's Red and Amber items laid on the reference network; Green is normal. A daily rating that matches this is the season; worse than this is a disruption. Hover a line or dot for its name.

How each item is scored

Each item is assessed on how long it hits freight at a stretch (under 3 days recovers easily; over a week is hard to recover), reach (local: in-city distribution or one cluster's pickups; regional: transhipment through a hub or an inter-city lane; national), severity (slight: absorbed; minor: slows freight; major: stops it, no practical detour) and nature. One rule turns that into a rating:

  • Red major, over a week, and beyond one city (regional or national)
  • Amber anything that is neither Red nor Green
  • Green slight; or under 3 days, unless national and major; or local, minor and under a week

Dates: the festival's own and, where an advisory gives them, the days roads or trade are hit (what the bulletin rates on). The Dussehra–Diwali season is Red nationally; on the maps and in the daily baseline each metro is graded from its past sale seasons (table below) and other hubs are Amber. How the bulletin uses this, and limits

Baseline first. A zone or lane that the calendar puts at Amber or Red, with nothing else happening, keeps that rating and the text says “as expected”. Seasonal strain is never Green. Disruption is the gap: a rating worse than the calendar, or a seasonal item arriving early, lasting longer or hitting harder, is called out. Quiet is not always good: a dip within the normal range is the season; a deeper one needs a cause. Ripple: a hub stays Amber about two days after its cause clears, three to five after a Red.

Not on the calendar: one-offs, rated as disruptions whatever the season. War and shipping lanes (Hormuz closure since February 2026; India–Middle East rates up 25–40% from late August[103]); bandhs (Odisha, 8 October 2026[104]); transport strikes (Delhi NCR chakka jam, May 2026[105]); earthquakes, collapses, hub fires; price and policy shocks. Lookalikes: Surat's March 2026 worker exodus came from an LPG shortage, not Holi[106]; check the cause, not the timing.

Volume index. Daily rate, one steady growth rate removed (B2B +17%, Trucks +2%, Rail +4%, Ports +7%, Containers +11% a year), each month's ratio to trend averaged over years, scaled to average 100. A month shows only with 2+ measured years (one year is that year's events); under 3 is indicative. Parcels: Delhivery's quarters, ratio to a centred moving average; only Delhivery reports enough; sale dates and complaints are never used for volume.

Data limits. Trucks: the 916 plazas reported every month (of 1,508); heavy = 3-axle and up; state expressways missing. B2B: bills over ₹50,000, a count not tonnes. Ports: twelve major ports only (no Mundra); tonnes mostly bulk. Rail: 4 months derived from published totals; two port months likewise. Calendar windows are the usual range; the road and trade dates, re-checked each edition, matter most.
