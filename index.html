INB India Network Bulletin · Edition 001 Tuesday 6 October 2026 · 11:30 IST

The sale opens on a clear network. The east is where it can break.

Sales open on Thursday[13,14]. Outside the east the network runs as in any sale season[12]. The east is Red: an Odisha bandh on Thursday[7], with Kolkata's truck curbs from Saturday to 27th October (Tuesday)[6] and a Bay low due around 13th October (Tuesday)[3]. The Gulf sea route stays shut[19].

Network: Amber Outlook window: Tue 6 – Sun 11 Oct

Decisions this week

  1. Sale volume reaches every metro hub once early access opens[13,14]. For carriers, the decision is how much sort and linehaul capacity to add before early access opens on 8th October (Thursday). Backlog carried into Thursday stays with the network into the peak, so the case for clearing hubs and pre-booking linehaul before early access opens is strong.
  2. Odisha's bandh and Kolkata's Mahalaya truck ban close eastern roads twice in the sale's first three days[7,6]. For shippers and carriers, the decision is which eastern loads move before the bandh on 8th October (Thursday) and which wait until after the ban on 10th October (Saturday). With both stoppages dated and Puja curbs to follow, moving them before the bandh carries the least risk.
  3. The Gulf route is shut and westbound air and ocean space is tightening into peak[19,21,23]. For exporters, the decision is how much Europe and US space to commit for peak-season orders while it is still available. With air rates to the US up 7% in a week[23], committing early is likely to cost less than waiting.
  4. Kolkata port trucks run four hours a day through the Puja days and stop for the immersions[6]. For importers, the decision is which Diwali-critical boxes must clear before the curbs begin on 13th October (Tuesday). With curbs and bans running until 27th October (Tuesday)[6], a box still at the port on the 13th faces two weeks of restricted movement.

Network insights

  1. The sale starts on a clear network. Outside the east, it will strain the way it does every year.

    High confidence

    Today traffic in ten big cities runs 1 to 6 points above usual[9] and JNPA turns trucks round in under two hours[10]. Amazon's sale and Flipkart's early access both open on Thursday[13,14]. Flipkart says it has added more than 900 festive delivery hubs[15] and more than 2.5 lakh direct and indirect seasonal job openings[16]. In the four weeks after each of the last four sale openings, searches for courier helplines rose 1.2 to 1.9 times in Delhi, Maharashtra and Karnataka, up to 2.8 times in Telangana and 2.2 in Tamil Nadu in single years, and 2.2 to 6.0 times in West Bengal[11,12].

    Outside the east, sale-week service will be decided in the first 72 hours by who starts with empty hubs. There is no outside disruption to blame there; any backlog will be self-inflicted.

  2. The east has no clean week until late October.

    High confidence

    This week Odisha has storms to Thursday[1] and a statewide bandh on Thursday[7], and Kolkata bars goods vehicles on Saturday. Puja curbs follow from 13 to 19th October (Monday) and immersion bans on 20–24 and 26th–27th October[6]. IMD expects a low over the Andaman Sea around 13th October (Tuesday), moving into the Bay, with a low chance of it becoming a depression[3]; cyclones are part of the east coast's season from October[12]. The trend is the concern: after each sale opening, West Bengal's helpline searches rose 2.2 times in 2022, 2.5 in 2023, 3.8 in 2024 and 6.0 in 2025[11].

    Eastern service for the whole festive season is being set this week, and Kolkata's sale-week strain is compounding year on year. It is structural; the weather only adds to it.

  3. The Gulf route stays shut, and westbound space is being bought up by sea and air.

    High confidence

    UKMTO reported five incidents near Hormuz on Monday, among them four tanker strikes since Saturday[18]. IMF PortWatch counted about 3 ships a day through the strait in the week to 27th September (Sunday), its latest published week, against 88 a day in September 2025[19]. India–Middle East container rates rose 25–40% from late August[20]. International air freight through Indian airports was 15.5% above last year in August and 14% above it from April to August[22], and India–US air tonnage is running 15% above last year[23].

    Peak-season space to Europe and the US will be rationed by price. Early commitment risks a rate that later softens; waiting risks no space for Diwali-season orders.

  4. Diesel has started to rise, at the private pumps first.

    Medium confidence

    Nayara raised diesel by ₹3 a litre at its 7,108 outlets from 3rd October (Saturday)[25], its second rise this year[26]. The state retailers, which run over 90% of India's pumps, have held prices, while ICRA estimated they lost about ₹9 a litre on diesel in September[25]; Delhi diesel is ₹95.20 today[24]. The Centre's excise on diesel has been nil since March, so it has no duty left to cut[27]. In May, more than 68 transport unions in Delhi-NCR struck for three days, citing fuel costs among their demands[28].

    A state-retailer rise during the festive weeks is now a real possibility, and transport unions have shown they will strike over fuel costs. The exposure sits in contracts with automatic diesel pass-through.

  5. Rail is the only mode adding capacity into the peak.

    Medium confidence

    The Western freight corridor has run from Dadri to JNPA since 8th September (Tuesday), with double-stack trains to the port[29]. Rail loading was 5.4% up in August, domestic containers 9.2%[30], yet only about 14% of JNPA's boxes went by rail in FY26[31]. Road capacity into Delhi tightens instead: at GRAP Stage III, BS-IV and older diesel light and medium goods vehicles from outside Delhi are restricted, and the exemption for BS-IV vehicles carrying essential goods ends on 31st October (Saturday)[32].

    Freight moved to the corridor now is capacity secured before late October, when Delhi's rules take older trucks out of the pool and every shipper is buying road space at once.

How this bulletin is rated

Ratings are editorial judgement on the likelihood of on-time delivery, built only from public sources: IMD forecasts, news on bandhs and road closures, police traffic plans, sale calendars, shipping trade press, fuel prices and official freight data. Air freight appears only with measured figures behind it: AAI's monthly airport freight and WorldACD's weekly market data. Every factual claim carries a numbered reference to the Sources list; anything that could not be checked against a source is left out.

Every rating is against what the seasonal calendar says is normal for the week. Green: running as the season normally does, which in sale or festival weeks can still be hard. Amber: a disruption beyond the season that slows freight. Red: one that stops freight across a state or a major corridor. A Red section of a lane downgrades the hubs at either end of that section. Disruption is carried forward: a hub or lane stays Amber for about two days after its cause clears, and longer after a Red. Exim flows also carry a cost rating, since delay hurts manufacturers and exporters far more than parcels.

Decisions this week are the few calls where missing the moment does damage that cannot be recovered within the season: three to five, soonest first, each stating the situation, whose decision it is and the event it must be made before. A view is given only where today's facts support one; each business knows its own network. Calls to check next week are scored in the next edition. Insights are listed by priority, then confidence. Confidence is High when the facts are dated and come from primary or measured sources, Medium when they rest on a proxy or a forecast. The cap, the order and the choice of calls are the owner's judgement.

Each metro's normal sale weeks come from past years (full table on the seasonal calendar), measured by Google searches for the Delhivery, Ekart, Blue Dart and DTDC helplines: these count customers chasing parcels, not late parcels or network load. Each city is read from its state (Karnataka for Bengaluru, Telangana for Hyderabad, West Bengal for Kolkata, Maharashtra for Mumbai, Tamil Nadu for Chennai); the sale opens on the day Amazon's festival opens to everyone; the rise is the busiest week in the four weeks after opening against a normal week in the eight before. These figures describe how hard a metro's season normally is; they set the baseline, not the colour.
